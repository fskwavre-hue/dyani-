Inconsistency almost never comes from a lack of goodwill. It comes from the absence of a written standard, from middle managers promoted without ever being trained to manage, and from new staff who learn the job by watching the person next to them.

Dyani Hospitality does not simply come in to train. We observe your services, identify where the experience breaks down, write the standards that are missing, train your teams to hold them, then verify on site that they still hold three months later.

The aim is not to make you dependent on a consultant. It is to leave behind a system that works without us.

Method

Seven stages. None optional.

Each stage produces a deliverable. It is their sequence that makes the result last.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Several services followed in real conditions, without intervening. What you see on a Friday night is in no document.

  2. 02

    Diagnose

    Where the experience breaks down, and why: a question of skill, or of structure.

  3. 03

    Structure

    Standards, procedures, checklists and role descriptions, in a format your teams will actually use.

  4. 04

    Train

    On site, in real situations, in the working language of the teams.

  5. 05

    Deploy

    Supervisors supported through the first services, where standards are usually lost.

  6. 06

    Measure

    Mystery visits, a gap-to-standard grid, indicators agreed with management.

  7. 07

    Refine

    Correcting what did not hold, adjusting procedures to the reality of the operation.

In the field

Where service takes place. Not in a seminar room.

With the teams who deliver it, on the equipment they actually use.

Read more

A team does not learn a standard by listening to it. It learns by performing it, corrected on the spot, until the gesture holds without having to think about it. This is why every assignment begins with several services observed in real conditions, not with a slide deck.

The documents handed over — procedures, checklists, role descriptions — are not end-of-assignment deliverables. They are the tools the teams work with during the training, and after we leave.

What has not been performed in front of me has not been trained.

Ibrahim Dyani

The profiles I train

Six levels, six sets of expectations. The content of a training session is not set by a job title, but by what the person has to hold during service.

01/ 06

Commis and waiters

Practical technique, service sequence, pace. The foundation: what has to be right before it can be fast.

Chefs de rang

Running a station, advising the guest, upselling. Selling without selling: informing well enough that the guest chooses better.

Maîtres d’hôtel

Directing service, coordinating with the kitchen, handling incidents. Holding the room when nothing goes to plan.

Floor managers

Briefing, checking, correcting, onboarding. This is often where everything is decided: training the supervisor improves every service that follows.

Front of house and reception

First seconds, bearing, complaint handling. The moment the impression is formed — and the moment it can be recovered.

Agency and event staff

Rapid levelling-up before an assignment, consistency of service. A team assembled for one evening has to hold the level of permanent staff.

His background

Training is not a career change; it has been part of my work since 2020. At JML Concept, official caterer to the Royal Palace of Belgium, I trained service teams to the requirements of protocol — including agency teams assembled for a single event who had to hold the same level as permanent staff.

I also train the managers, and that is often where everything is decided. A chef de rang promoted to maître d’hôtel knows how to serve, but no one has taught them to brief, to correct without humiliating, or to hold a standard through a full Saturday night.
